# Story Research

## Researcher: Build the Story Plan
1. Read the brief, audience, intended length and supplied sources. Clarify missing information that changes the topic or intended claim. If a verified plan already exists, use it without repeating unnecessary research.
2. Inspect relevant sources using available search/read tools. Prefer original records, primary reporting and named experts; cross-check disputed or consequential claims. Record author/publisher, title, URL, date and the supporting page/section/timecode. Search snippets alone are not verification. Report unavailable tools/access instead of inventing sources.
3. Select essential facts while preserving qualifications, chronology and context. Distinguish verified fact, attributed opinion, interpretation and unresolved uncertainty. A shortened account must not reverse the source's meaning.
4. Plan the narrative: hook -> context -> developments -> turning point -> conclusion. Do not fabricate conflict or imply causation from sequence alone. Adapt the structure to the topic rather than forcing a dramatic arc.
5. Deliver a Story Plan in details and/or a planning file. For each scene include objective, key talking points, transition to the next scene, estimated duration and claim/source IDs. List material omitted and why, including any context that must remain.

| Scene/time | Objective/talking points | Claim IDs | Source/location | Transition | Confidence/limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use short paraphrases; quote only when needed and attribute accurately. Match the language and tone requested by the user.

## Writer: Turn Evidence into a Script
- Read the Story Plan and its sources before writing dialogue. Preserve source IDs beside factual lines and retain qualifications in the spoken content where they affect meaning.
- Combine the factual narrative with the separate visual-reference analysis to produce spoken lines, visuals, on-screen text and timing for each scene. Do not introduce unsupported claims or portray invented speech as a real quote.
- Submit the Story Plan, references, script and storyboard for User Accept 1. Material factual/story changes require updated evidence and renewed approval.

## QA: Verify the Delivered Story
- Compare the actual script/preview with the approved plan and cited sources. Check factual claims, names, dates, quotations, chronology, context lost in cuts, and misleading visuals/captions.
- Report findings with scene/timecode, source evidence, impact and an actionable correction. Mark unverified checks rather than claiming success. Return factual/story changes to planning and User Accept 1; editing-only fixes may stay in the existing production/QA loop.

Content evidence and visual reference clips serve different purposes. Do not label an article as an inspected video or satisfy a visual-reference approval with a content citation. This skill does not launch agents, authorize publication or fine-tune a model.
